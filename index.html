This is a real volleyball court, filmed from above by a drone. The court is 18 metres long and 9 metres wide. The net, in orange, splits it into two squares of 9 by 9 metres. Three metres from the net on each side is the attack line; back-row players may not jump and attack in front of it. Around the court there is a free zone of at least 3 metres, and above it at least 7 metres of free space. The lines are 5 centimetres wide and belong to the court, so a ball on the line is in. Measured at the centre of the court

The net is measured in the middle of the court, from the floor to the top of the net. For men it is 2.43 metres, for women 2.24 metres, so the women’s net is 19 centimetres lower. The red and white rods are the antennas: they mark the sides of the court, and the ball must pass between them. Beach volleyball uses the same heights. For younger players the net is often lower, depending on the age group and the competition.

Libero = defensive specialist in a different colour

A team has six players on the court: three at the net and three in the back row. A squad can have up to 12 players, and at top international events up to 14, but then two of them must be liberos. The libero is a defensive specialist who plays only in the back row and wears a different colour. Each team can make six substitutions per set. Makes the second touch and sets up every attack.

Main attacker on the left; also receives the serve.

Blocks in the middle and hits fast attacks.

Opposite

Attacks from the right, opposite the setter.

Net ↑

Rotation — after winning back the serve, every player moves one zone clockwise

Each small court shows where a player stands at the start: the net is at the top. The setter makes the second touch and sets up the attacks. The two outside hitters attack from the left and also receive the serve. The middle blockers block in the middle of the net and hit fast attacks. The opposite plays opposite the setter and attacks from the right. The libero plays only in the back row, usually instead of a middle blocker, wears a different colour and may not serve, block or spike. When a team wins back the serve, every player rotates one zone clockwise.

These are the six basic skills. The serve starts every rally from behind the end line. The pass, or bump, is made with the forearms to receive the serve. The set is an overhead pass with the fingertips that prepares the ball for the attacker. The attack, or spike, is a jump and a hard hit down into the other court. The block is a jump at the net to stop the attack. And the dig saves a hard attack close to the floor. A team may touch the ball three times, usually pass, set and attack. Lithuania: in summer 2026 Šiauliai and Riga hosted the Women’s Under-18 European Championship together, and in 2024 Vilnius hosted the CEV Beach Volley Nations Cup, where Lithuania’s men’s pair won their group. Lithuanian clubs play in the national league and in the Baltic Volleyball League. Europe is the strongest volleyball continent: in 2025 Italy won both the men’s and the women’s World Championships, and France’s men won Olympic gold in Tokyo and in Paris. In the world: volleyball was invented in 1895 by William G. Morgan in Holyoke, USA; it has been an Olympic sport since 1964, beach volleyball since 1996, and more than 200 countries belong to the world federation, FIVB. Map data: Natural Earth.
